Parliamentary System

Module Polity – Volume I · Volume I source PDF · 25 MCQs
🗒 My notes for this chapter

Tap a question to reveal answer + reasoning. ✔ marks progress.

Part A Part B

Part A

Q1Consider the following statements:
1. The Council of Ministers includes Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State and Deputy Ministers.
2. The Cabinet is a larger body than the Council of Ministers.
3. Only Cabinet Ministers participate in cabinet meetings.
Which are correct?
  • A. 1 and 3 only
  • B. 1 only
  • C. 1 and 2 only
  • D. 2 and 3 only
Answer C
Q2The doctrine of collective responsibility implies:
Select the most appropriate answer.
  • A. Cabinet secrecy
  • B. Legislative supremacy
  • C. Political homogeneity
  • D. Fusion of powers
Answer C
Q3Which of the following situations would constitutionally require resignation of the Council of Ministers?
1. Defeat in No-Confidence Motion
2. Defeat in Money Bill
3. Defeat in Ordinary Bill
  • A. 1 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 1, 2 and 3
  • D. 2 only
Answer B
Q4Consider the following statements:
1. The Prime Minister must be a member of the Lok Sabha at the time of appointment.
2. A minister must become a member of Parliament within six months of appointment.
3. The Constitution explicitly defines the term “Cabinet.”
Which are correct?
  • A. 2 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer C
Q5Which of the following reflects “fusion of powers” in parliamentary system?
  • A. Executive independent of legislature
  • B. Executive drawn from legislature
  • C. Judiciary appointed by legislature
  • D. President elected by Parliament
Answer B
Q6In a coalition government, collective responsibility:
  • A. Ceases to operate
  • B. Applies only to major policies
  • C. Continues constitutionally
  • D. Is replaced by party-based voting
Answer C
Q7Which of the following is NOT a feature of parliamentary government?
  • A. Dual executive
  • B. Political homogeneity
  • C. Fixed executive tenure
  • D. Cabinet leadership
Answer C
Q8If the Prime Minister loses majority but refuses to resign, the President may:
  • A. Remove him immediately
  • B. Dissolve Lok Sabha automatically
  • C. Ask him to prove majority
  • D. Declare Emergency
Answer C
Q9Consider the following:
1. Cabinet is a constitutional body.
2. Council of Ministers is mentioned in Article 74.
3. Cabinet was inserted into Constitution by 44th Amendment.
Which are correct?
  • A. 2 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer C
Q10The term “responsible government” primarily refers to:
  • A. Accountability to judiciary
  • B. Accountability to people through referendum
  • C. Accountability of executive to legislature
  • D. Accountability of President to Cabinet
Answer C
Q11Which of the following statements is correct regarding dissolution of Lok Sabha?
  • A. President dissolves it at his discretion always
  • B. President must act on advice of Prime Minister
  • C. Rajya Sabha can veto dissolution
  • D. Supreme Court approves dissolution
Answer B
Q12In India, unlike the US presidential system:
1. Executive is accountable daily to legislature
2. There is separation of powers
3. Ministers are part of legislature
Which are correct?
  • A. 1 and 3 only
  • B. 1 only
  • C. 1, 2 and 3
  • D. 2 and 3 only
Answer A
Q13The stability–responsibility trade-off in parliamentary system implies:
  • A. Stable executive but weak legislature
  • B. Highly stable tenure
  • C. Greater accountability but potential instability
  • D. Presidential features
Answer C
Q14Which of the following most directly prevents despotism in parliamentary system?
  • A. Judicial review
  • B. Cabinet secrecy
  • C. No-confidence motion
  • D. Ordinance power
Answer C
Q15Which of the following correctly explains “Cabinet solidarity”?
  • A. Ministers cannot differ privately
  • B. Ministers cannot resign
  • C. Ministers must support decisions publicly
  • D. President approves cabinet decisions
Answer C
Q16Consider:
1. The Prime Minister can recommend dissolution of Lok Sabha.
2. The President may refuse dissolution if an alternative government is possible.
3. Dissolution power is absolute executive prerogative.
Which are correct?
  • A. 1 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer B
Q17Which of the following is a de facto executive?
  • A. President
  • B. Cabinet
  • C. Lok Sabha
  • D. Supreme Court
Answer B
Q18Loss of confidence in Lok Sabha leads to:
  • A. Automatic dissolution
  • B. Mandatory resignation or dissolution
  • C. Judicial intervention
  • D. Presidential veto
Answer B
Q19Which of the following best describes Indian parliamentary system?
  • A. Pure Westminster transplant
  • B. Presidential-parliamentary hybrid
  • C. Parliamentary system within federal framework
  • D. Unitary cabinet system
Answer C
Q20Which of the following would violate parliamentary principle?
  • A. Executive not answerable to legislature
  • B. PM drawn from legislature
  • C. Cabinet secrecy
  • D. Collective responsibility
Answer A

Part B

Q21In India, the President:
1. Is nominal executive
2. May act independently of advice in all matters
3. Is bound by advice after reconsideration
  • A. 1 only
  • B. 1 and 3 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer B
Q22Which situation tests collective responsibility most strongly?
  • A. Budget defeat
  • B. Rajya Sabha adjournment
  • C. Governor’s report
  • D. Presidential assent
Answer A
Q23Which of the following is true about Cabinet Committees?
  • A. Mentioned in Constitution
  • B. Headed only by President
  • C. Created for administrative efficiency
  • D. Have independent constitutional authority
Answer C
Q24Which of the following best captures the Indian deviation from British model?
  • A. Written Constitution
  • B. Ceremonial Head
  • C. Cabinet dominance
  • D. Prime Ministerial leadership
Answer A
Q25Which scenario would least likely disturb parliamentary accountability?
  • A. Hung Parliament
  • B. Coalition with outside support
  • C. Stable single-party majority
  • D. President’s rule
Answer C
