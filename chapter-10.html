Amendment of the Constitution

Part A

Q1
With reference to Article 368, consider the following statements:
1. The power of Parliament to amend the Constitution flows from Article 245.
2. The amendment power under Article 368 is a constituent power and not an ordinary legislative
power.
3. A Constitutional Amendment Bill requires prior recommendation of the President.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  • A. 2 only
  • B. 1 and 3 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer A
Q2Consider the following statements regarding Constitutional Amendment Bills:
1. They can be introduced in either House of Parliament.
2. They may be introduced by a private member.
3. They are subject to joint sitting in case of deadlock.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
  • A. 1 and 2 only
  • B. 2 and 3 only
  • C. 1 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer A
Q3Which of the following correctly describes “special majority” under Article 368?
  • A. Majority of total membership of the House only
  • B. Two-thirds of members present and voting only
  • C. Majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting
  • D. Two-thirds of total membership of the House
Answer C
Q4Consider the following provisions:
1. Election of the President
2. Supreme Court and High Courts
3. Distribution of legislative powers between Union and States
4. Official language provisions
Which of the above require ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures?
  • A. 1, 2 and 3 only
  • B. 1, 3 and 4 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2, 3 and 4
Answer D
Q5Which of the following can be amended by a simple majority of Parliament (outside Article 368)?
1. Creation of new States
2. Citizenship provisions
3. Delimitation of constituencies
4. Salaries of judges of Supreme Court
  • A. 1 and 3 only
  • B. 1, 2 and 3 only
  • C. 2 and 4 only
  • D. 1, 2, 3 and 4
Answer C
Q6Consider the following statements:
1. Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights after Kesavananda Bharati case.
2. Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights but cannot alter the Basic Structure.
3. Judicial review is part of the Basic Structure.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  • A. 2 and 3 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer C
Q7If Parliament passes a Constitutional Amendment Bill and the President withholds assent, which of the
following is correct?
  • A. Bill lapses
  • B. President may return it for reconsideration
  • C. President is constitutionally bound to give assent
  • D. Bill requires reintroduction
Answer B
Q8Consider the following statements:
1. There is no provision for joint sitting in case of disagreement on Constitutional Amendment Bill.
2. State Legislatures can amend the Constitution independently in federal matters.
3. States cannot initiate a Constitutional Amendment Bill.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  • A. 1 and 3 only
  • B. 2 only
  • C. 1 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer C
Q9Which of the following was inserted by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment regarding amendment power?
  • A. Removal of judicial review
  • B. Stated that constitutional amendments cannot be questioned in any court
  • C. Introduced referendum requirement
  • D. Provided veto power to States
Answer B
Q10The Minerva Mills case is significant because it:
  • A. Upheld unlimited amending power of Parliament
  • B. Struck down parts of 42nd Amendment expanding Article 31C
  • C. Declared Parliament sovereign
  • D. Removed Basic Structure doctrine
Answer B
Q11Which of the following would most likely violate the Basic Structure?
  • A. Amendment reducing tenure of Lok Sabha
  • B. Amendment abolishing judicial review
  • C. Amendment increasing number of Fundamental Duties
  • D. Amendment altering salary of judges
Answer B
Q12Consider the following:
1. Amendment procedure in India is rigid.
2. Amendment procedure in India is flexible.
3. Amendment procedure in India combines rigidity and flexibility.
Which is correct?
  • A. 1 only
  • B. 2 only
  • C. 3 only
  • D. 1 and 2 only
Answer C
Q13Which of the following require special majority but not state ratification?
1. Fundamental Rights
2. Directive Principles
3. Union Judiciary
4. Representation of States in Parliament
  • A. 1 and 2 only
  • B. 3 and 4 only
  • C. 1, 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2, 3 and 4
Answer B
Q14A Constitutional Amendment affecting federal provisions requires:
  • A. Special majority only
  • B. Special majority + ratification by half of states
  • C. Simple majority + ratification
  • D. Referendum
Answer B
Q15Which of the following correctly explains “constituent power”?
  • A. Power derived from legislative competence
  • B. Power to amend Constitution distinct from ordinary law-making
  • C. Power delegated by judiciary
  • D. Executive ordinance power
Answer B
Q16Consider the following:
1. Parliament may increase its own amending power by constitutional amendment.
2. Parliament may remove judicial review through constitutional amendment.
3. Parliament may amend Preamble.
Which of the above is/are correct?
  • A. 3 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 1 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer C
Q17Which of the following statements is correct?
  • A. Constitutional Amendment Bill must be introduced in Lok Sabha only
  • B. Rajya Sabha has no role in constitutional amendments
  • C. Both Houses must pass amendment separately
  • D. State Assemblies must approve all amendments
Answer C
Q18The expression “basic structure” appears explicitly in:
  • A. Article 368
  • B. Preamble
  • C. Supreme Court judgment
  • D. 44th Amendment
Answer C
Q19Which of the following is NOT part of Basic Structure?
  • A. Rule of Law
  • B. Parliamentary sovereignty
  • C. Federalism
  • D. Judicial review
Answer B
Q20Consider the following statements:
1. Ratification by states must occur before passage in Parliament.
2. Ratification requires simple majority in state legislatures.
3. There is no time limit prescribed for state ratification.
Which are correct?
  • A. 2 and 3 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 1 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer B

Part B

Q21Which of the following amendments altered the Preamble?
  • A. 42nd Amendment
  • B. 44th Amendment
  • C. 24th Amendment
  • D. 73rd Amendment
Answer A
Q22The 24th Constitutional Amendment primarily:
  • A. Introduced Basic Structure
  • B. Made President bound to give assent
  • C. Reduced state powers
  • D. Removed Article 368
Answer B
Q23If half the states ratify and later one state withdraws approval, what is the constitutional position?
  • A. Amendment fails
  • B. Withdrawal invalid
  • C. Fresh ratification required
  • D. President decides
Answer B
Q24Which of the following provisions can be amended without following Article 368?
1. Citizenship
2. Creation of Legislative Councils
3. Fifth Schedule
  • A. 1 and 2 only
  • B. 2 only
  • C. 1, 2 and 3
  • D. 3 only
Answer C
Q25Consider:
1. Amendment procedure in India resembles that of USA.
2. India does not provide referendum in amendment process.
3. States play equal role in amendment as Union.
Which are correct?
  • A. 2 only
  • B. 1 and 3 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer A
Q26Which statement best reflects post-Kesavananda position?
  • A. Parliament is supreme
  • B. Judiciary is supreme
  • C. Constitution is supreme subject to Basic Structure
  • D. States are sovereign
Answer C
Q27An amendment abolishing the Rajya Sabha would most likely:
  • A. Be valid if passed by special majority
  • B. Be valid with state ratification
  • C. Be invalid for violating federalism
  • D. Require referendum
Answer C
Q28Which of the following statements is incorrect?
  • A. Amendment Bill does not lapse on prorogation
  • B. Amendment Bill lapses on dissolution of Lok Sabha if pending there
  • C. Joint sitting is permitted
  • D. Special majority is required at all stages
Answer B
Q29Which of the following best explains India’s amendment model?
  • A. Completely rigid
  • B. Completely flexible
  • C. Hybrid federal amendment system
  • D. Presidential amendment model
Answer C
Q30Which scenario most clearly tests Basic Structure doctrine?
If this feels slightly uncomfortable and options look “almost correct” — good.
  • A. Altering salaries of MPs
  • B. Abolishing secularism
  • C. Increasing retirement age of judges
  • D. Expanding number of High Courts
Answer B
