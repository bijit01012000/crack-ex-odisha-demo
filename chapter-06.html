Part A

Q1The Constitution deals with citizenship under:
  • A. Articles 1–4
  • B. Articles 5–11
  • C. Articles 12–35
  • D. Article 368 only
Answer B
Q2The provisions in Articles 5–11:
1. Provide permanent and exhaustive rules of citizenship.
2. Apply only at the commencement of the Constitution.
3. Empower Parliament to legislate further.
How many statements are correct?
  • A. Only one
  • B. Only two
  • C. All three
  • D. None
Answer B
Q3Under Article 5, a person domiciled in India at commencement becomes citizen if:
1. Born in India
2. Either parent born in India
3. Resident in India for at least 5 years immediately before commencement
Which are correct?
  • A. 1 and 2 only
  • B. 1, 2 and 3
  • C. 2 only
  • D. 3 only
Answer B
Q4Article 6 deals with citizenship of persons migrating from:
  • A. Bangladesh
  • B. Pakistan
  • C. Nepal
  • D. Sri Lanka
Answer B
Q5Under Article 6, persons migrating before July 19, 1948 required:
  • A. Registration only
  • B. Naturalisation
  • C. Residence of 6 months
  • D. No registration requirement
Answer D
Q6Article 7 relates to:
  • A. Persons migrating to Pakistan
  • B. Persons of Indian origin abroad
  • C. Illegal migrants
  • D. Foreign diplomats
Answer A
Q7A person who migrated to Pakistan after March 1, 1947 but later returned under permit system:
  • A. Automatically became citizen
  • B. Required fresh naturalisation
  • C. Was treated under Article 6
  • D. Was permanently disqualified
Answer C
Q8Article 8 deals with citizenship of:
  • A. Refugees
  • B. Persons of Indian origin residing outside India
  • C. Illegal migrants
  • D. Stateless persons
Answer B
Q9Article 9 provides that citizenship is lost if a person:
  • A. Marries a foreigner
  • B. Voluntarily acquires foreign citizenship
  • C. Lives abroad for 7 years
  • D. Fails to vote
Answer B
Q10Article 11 empowers Parliament to regulate:
1. Acquisition of citizenship
2. Termination of citizenship
3. All matters relating to citizenship
  • A. 1 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 1, 2 and 3
  • D. 2 only
Answer B
Q11The Citizenship Act was enacted in:
  • A. 1950
  • B. 1952
  • C. 1955
  • D. 1960
Answer C
Q12Which of the following years saw amendments to the Citizenship Act?
1. 1986
2. 1992
3. 2003
4. 2015
  • A. 1 and 2 only
  • B. 1, 2 and 3 only
  • C. 1, 2, 3 and 4
  • D. 2 and 4 only
Answer C
Q13Citizenship by birth (post 2004 amendment) requires that:
  • A. Both parents must be citizens
  • B. At least one parent must not be illegal migrant
  • C. Either parent must be citizen
  • D. Child must register at 18
Answer B
Q14A person born in India between 1950 and 1987:
  • A. Is citizen only if both parents were citizens
  • B. Is citizen by birth irrespective of parents’ nationality
  • C. Must apply for citizenship
  • D. Is not automatically citizen
Answer B
Q15Citizenship by descent depends primarily on:
  • A. Place of birth
  • B. Citizenship of parents
  • C. Residence period
  • D. Registration in Parliament
Answer B
Q16Citizenship by registration can be granted to:
1. Persons of Indian origin
2. Spouse of Indian citizen
3. Minor children
  • A. 1 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 1, 2 and 3
  • D. 2 only
Answer C
Q17Naturalisation requires residence in India for:
  • A. 5 years
  • B. 7 years
  • C. 11 years
  • D. 12 months
Answer C
Q18Citizenship by incorporation of territory applies when:
  • A. State is bifurcated
  • B. Foreign territory becomes part of India
  • C. President declares so
  • D. Supreme Court directs
Answer B
Q19Loss of citizenship occurs by:
1. Renunciation
2. Termination
3. Deprivation
  • A. 1 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 1, 2 and 3
  • D. 2 only
Answer C
Q20Renunciation applies to:
  • A. Only naturalised citizens
  • B. Any citizen
  • C. Only citizens by birth
  • D. Only OCI holders
Answer B

  • A. Citizen commits offence
  • B. Citizen acquires foreign citizenship voluntarily
  • C. Citizen lives abroad
