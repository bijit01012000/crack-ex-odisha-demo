Part A

Q1Consider the following statements regarding evolution of the Basic Structure doctrine:
2. Kesavananda Bharati overruled Golak Nath on Parliament’s power to amend Fundamental Rights.
3. Kesavananda Bharati held that Parliament’s amending power is unlimited but judicially reviewable.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
  • A. 2 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer B
Q2Which of the following was the central constitutional question in Golak Nath case (1967)?
  • A. Whether Parliament can amend Directive Principles
  • B. Whether Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights
  • C. Whether President can veto constitutional amendments
  • D. Whether judiciary can review constitutional amendments
Answer B
Q3In Shankari Prasad (1951), the Supreme Court held that:
  • A. Constitutional amendments are “law” under Article 13
  • B. Constitutional amendments are not “law” under Article 13
  • C. Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights
  • D. Basic Structure limits amendment power
Answer B
Q4Which of the following best captures the holding in Kesavananda Bharati (1973)?
  • A. Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights at all
  • B. Parliament can amend any provision, including Basic Structure
  • C. Parliament can amend any provision except Basic Structure
  • D. Judiciary has no role in amendment review
Answer C
Q5Consider the following features:
1. Judicial review
2. Supremacy of Parliament
3. Federal character
4. Secularism
Which of the above have been recognized as part of Basic Structure?
  • A. 1, 3 and 4 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2, 3 and 4
Answer A
Q6In Indira Nehru Gandhi case (1975), the Supreme Court invalidated a constitutional amendment primarily
because it:
  • A. Violated separation of powers
  • B. Curtailed Fundamental Duties
  • C. Eliminated election disputes from judicial review
  • D. Expanded Parliament’s tenure
Answer C
Q7Which of the following cases reaffirmed harmony between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles as
part of Basic Structure?
  • A. Golak Nath
  • B. Minerva Mills
  • C. Shankari Prasad
  • D. Sajjan Singh
Answer B
Q8Consider the following statements:
1. Basic Structure is defined exhaustively in the Constitution.
2. Basic Structure doctrine emerged through judicial interpretation.
3. Parliament can expand the list of Basic Structure elements through amendment.
Which are correct?
  • A. 2 only
  • B. 1 and 2 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer C
Q9Which of the following would most clearly violate the Basic Structure doctrine?
  • A. Increasing retirement age of judges
  • B. Abolishing judicial review
  • C. Changing number of High Court judges
  • D. Modifying Schedule entries
Answer B
Q10The term “constituent power” under Article 368 refers to:
  • A. Power delegated by States
  • B. Ordinary legislative power
  • C. Power to amend Constitution subject to Basic Structure
  • D. Power of judiciary
Answer C
Q11Consider the following:
1. Rule of Law
2. Free and fair elections
3. Parliamentary sovereignty
4. Unity and integrity of India
Which are part of Basic Structure?
  • A. 1, 2 and 4 only
  • B. 1 and 3 only
  • C. 2 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2, 3 and 4
Answer A
Q12Minerva Mills case (1980) primarily limited Parliament’s power by:
  • A. Reversing Kesavananda
  • B. Striking down expansion of Article 31C
  • C. Removing judicial review
  • D. Declaring FR superior to DPSP absolutely
Answer B
Q13Which statement best describes post-Kesavananda constitutional position?
  • A. Parliament is supreme
  • B. Judiciary is supreme
  • C. Constitution is supreme subject to Basic Structure
  • D. States are sovereign
Answer C
Q14If Parliament amends Article 368 to remove judicial review over amendments, such amendment would most
likely be:
  • A. Valid
  • B. Valid if states ratify
  • C. Invalid for violating Basic Structure
  • D. Subject to referendum
Answer C
Q15Consider the following statements:
1. Basic Structure applies only to Fundamental Rights.
2. Basic Structure limits Parliament’s constituent power.
3. Directive Principles can form part of Basic Structure.
Which are correct?
  • A. 2 only
  • B. 2 and 3 only
  • C. 1 and 3 only
  • D. 1, 2 and 3
Answer B
Q16Which of the following cases introduced the concept that FR and DPSP should be harmoniously construed?
  • A. Kesavananda Bharati
  • B. Minerva Mills
  • C. Golak Nath
  • D. Sajjan Singh
Answer B
Q17Which of the following is NOT generally accepted as Basic Structure?
  • A. Republican form of government
  • B. Supremacy of Constitution
  • C. Power of Parliament to amend without limitation
  • D. Secular character
Answer C
Q18A constitutional amendment replacing India’s federal system with a unitary one would most likely:
  • A. Be valid if ratified by half of states
  • B. Be valid under special majority
  • C. Be invalid as it affects Basic Structure
  • D. Require referendum
Answer C
Q19Which of the following was specifically declared part of Basic Structure in Kesavananda case?
  • A. Directive Principles
  • B. Judicial review
  • C. Adult suffrage
  • D. Panchayati Raj
Answer C
Q20Consider the following statements:
1. Basic Structure doctrine was a judicial innovation.

  • A. 1 only
